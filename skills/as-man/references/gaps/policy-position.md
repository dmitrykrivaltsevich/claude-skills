# Gaps — policy, regulation, ethics code, position statement

Applies to: proposed and enacted regulation, ethics codes, institutional position statements, advocacy documents.

## Expected coverage

| Probe | What a reader needs |
|---|---|
| Interest served | Who benefits if this is adopted, and who wrote it. |
| Strongest counter-argument | The best case against, stated as its holders would state it. A position document almost never contains this. |
| Enforcement | What happens when the rule is broken, and who acts. A rule with no mechanism is a statement of preference. |
| Binding scope | Who is bound, who is exempt, and where it has no jurisdiction. |
| Compliance cost | What it costs to comply, and whether that cost falls hardest on the smallest actors. |
| Definitions | Which terms carry the load, and how much the document's effect depends on where those lines fall. |
| Timeline | When obligations begin, and what applies to things that already exist. |
| Displacement | Whether the regulated behaviour moves elsewhere rather than stopping. |
| Prior attempts | Whether something similar was tried before, and what happened. |
| Measurement | How anyone would know whether it worked. |

## Genre traps

- **Aspiration reads as obligation.** Principles documents state values with no mechanism; a summary that omits this converts a hope into a rule.
- **The consultation shaped the text.** Which parties were consulted often explains which exemptions exist.
- **Definitions are where the fight happened.** A threshold or a definition frequently determines the whole effect.
- **Moral framing crowds out mechanism.** A document can be entirely right about what matters and silent on what to do.
- **Institutional statements bind nobody.** Note explicitly when a document has no enforcement power.

Treat the document's own moral position as data, not as something to endorse or refute. Report what it omits; do not take a side on the question it addresses.

## Example

```
GAP 1 - No enforcement mechanism is specified

Kind:         omission
Materiality:  high
Missing:      The document states obligations but names no authority
              responsible for enforcement and no consequence for breach.
Effect:       A reader treats the obligations as binding and plans compliance
              work that no counterparty is actually required to match.
Evidence:     [unverified - model knowledge, confirm before acting]
```
