# Gaps — news article, blog post, press release

Applies to: news reporting, opinion pieces, corporate announcements, personal blog posts.

## Expected coverage

| Probe | What a reader needs |
|---|---|
| Base rate | How often this happens normally. Without it, any single event sounds like a trend. |
| Denominator | The absolute numbers behind every proportion. "Up 200%" from two to six is not news. |
| Prior events | What came before, and whether this is a continuation or a change. |
| Beneficiary | Who gains if the reader believes this, and who supplied the story. |
| Sourcing | Whether claims are attributed, and what "sources familiar with the matter" means here. |
| The other side | Whether the affected party was asked, and what they said. |
| Expertise | Whether quoted experts have relevant expertise and any interest in the outcome. |
| Study behind the story | For science reporting, what the underlying paper actually measured and concluded. |
| Correlation | Whether a causal claim rests on correlational evidence. |
| Timing | Why this is published now, especially around funding, launches, or regulation. |
| Correction history | Whether the piece has been updated since publication. |

## Genre traps

- **Headline and body disagree.** The headline is written to be clicked and frequently overstates the reporting.
- **A press release rewritten as news.** Announcements often reach the reader with the vendor's framing intact.
- **Science reporting through a press office.** The chain from paper to press release to article loses caveats at every step. Go to the paper.
- **Anecdote as trend.** Two cases and a quote.
- **Absent uncertainty.** Forecasts and estimates reported as facts.

## Example

```
GAP 1 - The percentage has no denominator

Kind:         omission
Materiality:  high
Missing:      The reported 300% increase covers a rise from 4 incidents to 16
              across a population of roughly 90 000.
Effect:       A reader treats a small absolute change as a significant trend
              and reprioritises work on the strength of it.
Evidence:     [unverified - model knowledge, confirm before acting]
```
