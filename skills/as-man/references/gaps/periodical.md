# Gaps — magazine or journal issue

Applies to: whole issues of magazines and journals, newsletters, curated collections.

## Expected coverage

| Probe | What a reader needs |
|---|---|
| Editorial selection | What the issue's theme excludes, and whether the exclusion changes the picture. |
| Commissioning | Who chose the contributors, and whether they share a position. |
| Sponsored content | Which items are advertising, and whether that is marked clearly. |
| Article-level support | Claims stated confidently in a short article that its length cannot support. |
| Recurring position | Whether the publication has run this argument before, and whether it has run the counter-argument. |
| Community scope | Which community the publication speaks for, and whose work is therefore absent. |
| Time lag | The delay between writing and publication, which for print can be months. |
| Missing dissent | Whether any item in the issue disagrees with any other. Uniformity in a themed issue is a signal. |

## Genre traps

- **A themed issue looks like a survey and is not.** Ten articles chosen to fit a theme read as evidence that the theme matters.
- **The practitioner and academic split.** Many publications draw from one and describe the other second-hand.
- **Regional and language scope.** An English-language publication under-reports work published elsewhere.
- **Front-of-book opinion reads as reporting.** Editorials and columns carry the publication's authority without its standards.

At issue level, a gap is usually about selection rather than fact. At article level, route to the genre of the individual article instead: a research summary inside a magazine is best probed with `research-paper.md`.

## Example

```
GAP 1 - The issue contains no dissenting article

Kind:         conflict-of-interest
Materiality:  medium
Missing:      All seven feature articles argue in favour of the theme. The
              established critique of the approach appears in none of them.
Effect:       A reader treats the issue as a survey of the field and concludes
              the question is settled when it is actively disputed.
Evidence:     [unverified - model knowledge, confirm before acting]
```
